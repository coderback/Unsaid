01 The question

Cohen, Malloy and Nguyen (Journal of Finance, 2020) found that companies which change the language of their annual report earn lower subsequent returns than companies whose filings barely move. Buy the quiet filers, short the changers: 34–58 basis points a month, value-weighted. The intuition is that managers rewrite disclosure when something has changed, and the market is slow to read it.

This study asked whether that holds in one sector where the stakes are unusually legible. Banks fail in a specific, documented way — interest-rate risk that stops being disclosed shortly before it matters — and Silicon Valley Bank supplied the motivating case. The universe is 57 US banks; the measure is cosine similarity between consecutive Item 1A (Risk Factors) sections; the test is whether low-change filers outperform high-change filers over the following year.

02 What the in-sample data said

Over FY2019–2023 the top-versus-bottom quintile spread on Item 1A cosine similarity at twelve months was +7.98%, t = 2.48 across 197 pairs. Right sign, right section, right horizon, conventionally significant. It looked like a replication.

It had a tell. The median split — same signal, same horizon, just a coarser cut — came in at +0.16%, t = 0.06. An effect concentrated entirely in the extreme quintiles and absent from the broad comparison is the shape of a few outliers, not of a pervasive anomaly.

03 Why that was not enough

That +7.98% was not the only thing measured. It was one cell in a grid of 24 specifications: four similarity measures, several sections, four horizons, two directions. Reporting the best cell from a search that size and calling it significant is the standard way to find something that is not there.

A permutation test asks the right question — not “how unlikely is this t-statistic?” but “how often does a search this wide throw up a t-statistic this large somewhere, when nothing is going on?” Shuffling returns against signals and re-running the whole grid gave a family-wise p = 0.131. The result sat inside the range of noise.

What correction can and cannot do. A family-wise p-value establishes that a result is consistent with chance. It cannot establish whether this particular result is real. Only new data separates those.

04 The pre-registered test

Every number above came from FY2019–2023. The study had stopped there for no reason: the universe file was written in August 2026, by which point the FY2024 10-Ks (filed February 2025) and FY2025 10-Ks (filed February 2026) had both existed for months. Extending forward produced 103 new pairs, and among them one that mattered more than the rest — FY2023→24, a genuine holdout with complete twelve-month returns that no specification search had touched.

A holdout you search is not a holdout, so the test was written down and committed to git before extraction finished: signal sim_1a, horizon 12M, median split, long the quiet filers, one test. The interpretation of each outcome was fixed at the same time, so the reading could not drift once a number was visible: similar magnitude and sign would be a genuine replication, near zero would mean the in-sample result was an artifact, and negative would carry the same conclusion as near zero.

Amendment, made before the number was computed

A returns-blind quality check found five holdout pairs where “Item 1A” was a cross-reference pointer, not the section — BK 9 words, USB 17, WFC 20. Two identical pointers score a perfect 1.0000, which sorts them to the very top of the quiet-filer ranking, first in line on the long side of this exact test.

The filter requires 2,500 words of Item 1A on both sides. Applied to the already-spent in-sample data it cost about 0.65pp and did not manufacture the result. Declared and committed ahead of the holdout figure.

06 Then the instrument was repaired

Four large banks — Wells Fargo, US Bancorp, BNY Mellon, Morgan Stanley — were returning 20, 17, 9 and 189 words for Item 1A. The cause was not a parsing failure. Each filing says plainly where the section is:

“Information in response to this Item 1A can be found in the 2024 Annual Report to Shareholders under ‘Financial Review – Risk Factors.’”

The prose is filed as Exhibit 13, a separate document in the same filing, and the extractor only ever read the primary one. Following the pointer recovers it:

Eighteen pairs recovered, their cosines moving from a degenerate 1.0000 to a real 0.92–0.997. In the other direction, Morgan Stanley and Citizens were found to have “Item 1A” extractions of 584,000–656,000 characters — the entire back half of the document. Those produced plausible 0.99 cosines because the over-capture was consistent year to year, but they were never Item 1A, and eight such pairs were dropped.

In-sample data quality went from 184 of 224 pairs carrying a genuine Item 1A to 213 of 218. And the in-sample result got weaker:

This is the third independent line of evidence, and arguably the most telling. The result had already failed multiplicity correction; it now also falls below the naive significance threshold. An effect that shrinks as measurement error is removed is behaving like measurement error.

07 What this does and does not establish

Stated precisely, the holdout is a failure to confirm, not a demonstrated negative. With 45 pairs and a return standard deviation near 15%, its own minimum detectable effect is roughly 8.8% at 95% confidence. It could never have detected the 2–3.5% effect the original paper reports, and could only marginally have detected the in-sample figure that made this worth pursuing.

What the three findings together establish is that the effect did not survive correction for the search that found it, did not reappear out of sample, and shrank when the instrument improved. No one of those settles it. Together they do.

The numbers that are not evidence

The FY2024→25 pairs came back positive at short horizons: +3.02% at three months (t = 1.49) and +3.52% at six (t = 1.32), on 47 pairs.

Not part of the test

These sit at horizons that were never registered, on a pair-year whose twelve-month returns do not exist until February 2027. Reading them as rescuing the result would be exactly the specification search the holdout was built to rule out.

They are reported because omitting them would be selective too. They change nothing.

08 Item 7A, and why it was never testable here

Item 7A — Quantitative and Qualitative Disclosures About Market Risk — is where a bank's interest-rate sensitivity tables live, and it is the section the original thesis leaned on. It cannot carry that weight on this universe: roughly two thirds of issuers file no standalone Item 7A, routing market risk into their own MD&A and leaving a twenty-word pointer.

A late finding softened this slightly without changing it. Some issuers point not at their own MD&A but at the Annual Report exhibit, which is followable the way Item 1A now is — about five or six of them. Recovering those would take the usable 7A sample from 39 pairs to perhaps 70. The minimum detectable effect at that size is still near 7% against a 2–3.5% target, so the section stays untestable here; it merely becomes less thinly untestable.

Silicon Valley Bank, which had a real Item 7A with a real sensitivity table, is the exception that made the whole approach look more general than it is.

09 Where this leaves the study

The disclosure-change anomaly does not replicate on this banking universe. That is the finding, and it was reached through a pre-registered test rather than by running out of ideas.

It also reprices the obvious next step. Extending back to FY2010 would take the sample to roughly 855 pairs and the minimum detectable effect to 3.2% — the first point at which the original effect size becomes detectable at all. Before the holdout that was the clear move. After it, it buys statistical power for an effect that has failed its out-of-sample test and shrunk under better measurement. That is a materially worse bet, and worth saying plainly rather than proceeding on momentum.

Three constraints would bind any continuation, independent of sample size:

  • Time clustering. Banks file within weeks of each other every February, so 319 pairs represent roughly six independent periods, not 319 independent observations.
  • Extraction fidelity. The instrument was measuring the wrong text for a meaningful fraction of the corpus, concentrated at the extreme of the ranking that drives the result. It is better now; it was never checked before.
  • Sector choice. A universe of banks maximises thematic relevance and minimises independence. The original result draws on thousands of firms filing year-round.
On the negative result. The version of this study that reported +7.98%, t = 2.48 and stopped would have been wrong, and nothing in the in-sample data alone would have revealed it. What revealed it was correcting for the search, testing on data the search had not seen, and then fixing the instrument and watching the effect shrink. All three were available before publication. Only the first is conventional.
