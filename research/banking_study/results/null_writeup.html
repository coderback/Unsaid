01 The question

Cohen, Malloy and Nguyen (Journal of Finance, 2020) found that companies which change the language of their annual report earn lower subsequent returns than companies whose filings barely move. Buy the quiet filers, short the changers: 34–58 basis points a month, value-weighted. The intuition is that managers rewrite disclosure when something has changed, and the market is slow to read it.

This study asked whether that holds in one sector where the stakes are unusually legible. Banks fail in a specific, documented way — interest-rate risk that stops being disclosed shortly before it matters — and Silicon Valley Bank supplied the motivating case. The universe is 57 US banks; the measure is cosine similarity between consecutive Item 1A (Risk Factors) sections; the test is whether low-change filers outperform high-change filers over the following year.

02 What the in-sample data said

Over FY2019–2023, the top-versus-bottom quintile spread on Item 1A cosine similarity at a twelve-month horizon was +7.98% with a t-statistic of 2.48 across 197 pairs. Right sign, right section, right horizon, conventionally significant. It looked like a replication.

It also had a tell. The median split — the same signal, same horizon, just a coarser cut — came in at +0.16%, t = 0.06. An effect concentrated entirely in the extreme quintiles and absent from the broad comparison is the shape of a small number of outliers, not the shape of a pervasive anomaly.

03 Why that was not enough

That +7.98% was not the only thing measured. It was one cell in a grid of 24 specifications: four similarity measures, several sections, four horizons, two directions. Reporting the best cell from a search of that size and calling it significant is the standard way to find something that is not there.

A permutation test answers the right question — not “how unlikely is this t-statistic?” but “how often does a search this wide throw up a t-statistic this large somewhere, when nothing is going on?” Shuffling the returns against the signals and re-running the entire grid gave a family-wise p = 0.131. Thirteen percent of random draws produce a hit at least this good. The result was inside the range of noise.

What correction can and cannot do. A family-wise p-value establishes that a result is consistent with chance. It cannot establish whether this particular result is real. Those are different claims, and only new data can separate them.

04 The pre-registered test

Every number above came from FY2019–2023. The study had stopped there for no reason: the universe file was written in August 2026, by which point the FY2024 10-Ks (filed February 2025) and FY2025 10-Ks (filed February 2026) had both existed for months.

Extending forward produced 103 new pairs, and among them one that mattered more than the rest. FY2023→24 is a genuine holdout — 53 pairs with complete twelve-month returns that no specification search had ever touched.

A holdout you search is not a holdout, so the test was written down first and committed to git before extraction finished:

One amendment, also made before the number was computed

A quality check on the extracted text — which reads word counts only and never touches returns — found five holdout pairs where “Item 1A” was not Item 1A at all:

A real Risk Factors section runs 60,000–160,000 characters. These are cross-reference pointers the parser returned in place of the section, and two identical pointers score a perfect 1.0000 — which sorts them to the very top of the quiet-filer ranking, first in line on the long side of this exact test.

The filter requires at least 2,500 words of Item 1A on both sides. Applied to the already-spent in-sample data it moves the quintile from +7.98% to +7.33% and the median split from +0.16% to −0.58%: it costs about 0.65pp and does not manufacture the result. Those filtered figures are the like-for-like comparators used throughout.

06 What this does and does not establish

Stated precisely, this is a failure to confirm, not a demonstrated negative. With 45 pairs and a return standard deviation near 15%, the holdout's own minimum detectable effect is roughly 8.8% at 95% confidence. It could never have detected the 2–3.5% effect the original paper reports, and could only marginally have detected the +7.33% that made the in-sample result look interesting.

What it does establish is that the effect did not reappear at anything like the magnitude that made it worth pursuing — and that two independent lines of evidence now point the same way. The in-sample result failed multiplicity correction; the out-of-sample test reversed. Neither alone would settle it. Together they do.

The number that is not evidence

The FY2024→25 pairs, added in the same extension, came back positive at short horizons: +4.00% at three months (t = 1.84) and +4.65% at six months (t = 1.73), on 48 pairs.

Not part of the test

These sit at horizons that were never registered, on a pair-year whose twelve-month returns do not exist until February 2027. Reading them as rescuing the result would be exactly the specification search the holdout was built to rule out.

They are reported because leaving them out would be selective too. They change nothing.

07 What broke along the way

Two findings about the instrument matter more for future work than the null itself.

Item 7A is untestable on this universe

Item 7A — Quantitative and Qualitative Disclosures About Market Risk — is where a bank's interest-rate sensitivity tables live, and it is the section the original thesis leaned on. Measured across the universe, it splits three ways:

Two thirds of the universe route market risk into the MD&A and leave a twenty-word pointer behind. No extractor recovers a section that was never filed separately, which caps the 7A thesis at roughly 56 observations against the ~725 the power analysis requires. It is not underpowered; it is untestable here. Silicon Valley Bank, which had a real Item 7A with a real sensitivity table, is the exception that made it look otherwise.

Extraction quality was a live confound, not a footnote

Across the corpus, 7.6% of in-sample pairs and 9.4% of holdout pairs had an “Item 1A” that was a pointer rather than the section. Because two identical pointers score 1.0000, these landed at the top of the quiet-filer ranking — noise in precisely the position that most affects every result in the study.

The extractor now enforces per-section bounds. The two sections fail in opposite directions, so the bounds are deliberately asymmetric: Item 1A is never legitimately short, while a short Item 7A is usually correct. The floor is sited in an empty band measured across 401 cached filings — 27 extractions at or below 2,181 characters, 374 at or above 18,696, and nothing in between.

This does not repair the affected filings. For four institutions the parser fails on the document structure entirely and no fallback recovers it, so the honest outcome is no answer rather than a wrong one: 20 of 327 pairs lose Item 1A altogether.

08 Where this leaves the study

The disclosure-change anomaly does not replicate on this banking universe. That is the finding, and it is a real one — arrived at through a pre-registered test rather than by running out of ideas.

It also reprices the obvious next step. Extending the corpus back to FY2010 would take the sample to roughly 855 pairs and the minimum detectable effect to 3.2%, which is the first point at which the original paper's effect size becomes detectable at all. Before the holdout, that was the clear move. After it, it buys statistical power for an effect that has just failed its one out-of-sample test — a materially worse bet, and worth saying plainly rather than proceeding on momentum.

Three constraints would bind any continuation, independent of sample size:

  • Time clustering. Banks file within a few weeks of each other every February, so 327 pairs represent roughly six independent periods, not 327 independent observations.
  • Extraction fidelity. The instrument was measuring the wrong text for about 8% of the corpus, concentrated at the extreme of the ranking that drives the result.
  • Sector choice. A universe of banks maximises thematic relevance and minimises independence. The original result is drawn from thousands of firms filing year-round.
On the negative result. Every number here was produced by a pipeline whose defects are documented above, and the honest summary of the strongest cut is that it reversed out of sample. That is worth more than a positive result from the same pipeline would have been — the version of this study that reported +7.98%, t = 2.48 and stopped would have been wrong, and nothing in the in-sample data alone would have revealed it.
