## REMOVED

### 1. Boston Private Acquisition — Impact on EVE and NII Sensitivity
`ITEM 7A` · conf 82%

**Year-1 Disclosure**
> The acquisition of Boston Private was completed in July 2021... Both the EVE and NII sensitivity measures described here are inclusive of the impact of the Boston Private acquisition.

**Year-2 Status**
> ✕ No corresponding disclosure found in Year-2 filing

**Analysis**
The Year-2021 unit specifically disclosed that the EVE/NII sensitivity measures incorporated the Boston Private acquisition's ~$10B assets and ~$9B deposits. None of the Year-2022 candidates reference Boston Private or any acquisition-driven impact on the sensitivity measures; the 2022 disclosures address generic drivers (funding mix, deposit beta, swap termination), not the acquisition. By Year-2 the acquisition is fully integrated and no longer a distinct disclosure item.

---

### 2. EVE Base — Balance Sheet Growth and Composition Changes Year-over-Year
`ITEM 7A` · conf 78%

**Year-1 Disclosure**
> Our base EVE as of December 31, 2021 increased $9.5 billion from December 31, 2020, driven by overall balance sheet growth as well as yield curve steepening during the first half of 2021.

**Year-2 Status**
> ✕ No corresponding disclosure found in Year-2 filing

**Analysis**
The Year-2021 unit is a specific quantitative narrative explaining the year-over-year change in base EVE attributable to concrete balance sheet growth and composition shifts (cash, AFS, HTM, loans, deposit mix). None of the Year-2022 candidates provides an analogous EVE base-change walk-forward; they cover modeling methodology, NII sensitivity drivers, deposit beta, benchmark indexes, and static-balance-sheet methodology. The specific economic disclosure of the EVE base balance-sheet-driven change is genuinely absent.

### 3. EVE Hedging — Interest Rate Swaps and Sensitivity Reduction
`ITEM 7A` · conf 86%

**Year-1 Disclosure**
> In March 2021 we purchased interest rate swaps to offset some of the additional EVE sensitivity... The addition of pay fixed swaps reduces this exposure to -13.9 percent in the same scenario.

**Year-2 Status**
> termination of our pay fixed swaps portfolio

**Analysis**
The Year-1 disclosure described an active EVE hedge using pay fixed swaps that reduced rate-shock sensitivity; Year-2 explicitly states the pay fixed swaps portfolio was terminated, so the hedge-based EVE mitigation no longer exists and there is no replacement EVE hedging disclosure. The risk-mitigating activity is genuinely gone.

### 4. 12-Month NII Base Forecast — Year-over-Year Growth Drivers
`ITEM 7A` · conf 72%

**Year-1 Disclosure**
> Our base case static 12-month NII forecast at December 31, 2021 increased compared to December 31, 2020 by $1.9 billion, driven primarily by the growth in the balance sheet that has taken place year-to-date.

**Year-2 Status**
> ✕ No corresponding disclosure found in Year-2 filing

**Analysis**
The Year-1 unit discloses the absolute year-over-year change in the base-case static NII forecast ($1.9B) and its drivers (balance sheet growth, fixed income deployment, high cash position). No Year-2 candidate quantifies or discusses the YoY change in the base-case NII level or its growth drivers; Candidate 1 instead addresses drivers of change in NII rate sensitivity, which is a distinct economic risk (rate exposure, not base-level forecast growth).

## SOFTENED

### 5. EVE and NII Sensitivity Table — Rate Shock Scenarios 2021 vs. 2020
`ITEM 7A` · conf 72%

**Year-1 Disclosure**
> December 31, 2021: +200 ... EVE $(5,722) (27.7)% ... NII $981 22.9%; +100 ... EVE $(2,873) (13.9)% ... NII $468 10.9%

**Year-2 Status**
> December 31, 2022: +200 | 3.5%; +100 | 1.8% — NII sensitivity only; EVE column eliminated

**Analysis**
The Year-2022 table drops the EVE sensitivity disclosure entirely (no longer quantifying the large -27.7%/-13.9% EVE declines) and shows NII asset sensitivity collapsing from +22.9%/+10.9% to +3.5%/+1.8%, with Candidate 2 confirming a deliberate reduction in asset sensitivity. The same interest-rate risk persists but is materially reduced and the EVE dimension is no longer quantified, so the disclosed risk is downgraded/hedged.

### 6. Economic Value of Equity — Valuation Methodology and Limitations
`ITEM 7A` · conf 72%

**Year-1 Disclosure**
> We are exposed to yield curve risk, prepayment risk, basis risk and yield spread compression, which cannot be fully modeled and expressed using the above methodology. Accordingly, the results in the preceding table should not be relied upon as a precise indicator of actual results...

**Year-2 Status**
> Rate sensitivity measures the potential variability in economic value and NII relating solely to changes in market interest rates over time. ... These assumptions may change in future periods based on changes in client behavior and at management's discretion.

**Analysis**
The Year-2 disclosures retain the assumption-dependence and methodology description, but the explicit, named enumeration of un-modelable risks (yield curve, prepayment, basis, yield spread compression) and the pointed caveats that results "should not be relied upon as a precise indicator" and "should not be construed to represent our estimate" of EVE/NII are no longer present. The candidates describe methodology and assumptions but drop the specific limitations/caveat language, weakening the risk disclosure.

### 7. EVE Sensitivity — Excess Deposits, Investment Portfolio Mix and Rate Shock Impact
`ITEM 7A` · conf 70%

**Year-1 Disclosure**
> a significant amount of excess deposits not used to fund loan growth have contributed to the growth of investment balances... under an upward rate shock scenario, the market value of investments changes more than the market value of deposits resulting in a negative EVE sensitivity in those scenarios.

**Year-2 Status**
> The reduction in our NII asset sensitivity for each parallel rate shock scenario above is primarily driven by the shift in our funding mix towards interest-bearing deposits and short-term borrowings... termination of our pay fixed swaps portfolio and the extension of our fixed income portfolio as a result of higher rates, partially offset by growth in variable rate loans.

**Analysis**
The Year-1 disclosure explicitly identified a specific, concrete risk: excess deposits driving a fixed-rate investment portfolio that produces negative EVE sensitivity under upward rate shocks. In Year-2, no candidate restates this negative-EVE-from-excess-deposits/fixed-rate-MBS risk; the closest (Candidate 1) discusses general drivers of reduced NII asset sensitivity and frames portfolio extension neutrally rather than flagging the heightened market-value vulnerability. The specific adverse risk framing is downgraded/hedged into generic sensitivity commentary.

### 8. 12-Month NII Sensitivity — Rate Shock Scenario Comparisons 2021 vs. 2020
`ITEM 7A` · conf 86%

**Year-1 Disclosure**
> At December 31, 2021, NII sensitivity was 10.9 percent in the +100 bps interest rate scenario... Our NII sensitivity in the +200 bps interest rate shock scenario was 22.9 percent... -100 bps scenario of negative 6.4 percent... -200 bps scenario... negative 8.6 percent.

**Year-2 Status**
> December 31, 2022: +200 | 3.5%; +100 | 1.8% ... The reduction in our NII asset sensitivity for each parallel rate shock scenario above is primarily driven by the shift in our funding mix towards interest-bearing deposits...

**Analysis**
The same NII sensitivity risk disclosure is retained with the same methodology and table structure, but the measured asset sensitivity dropped sharply (+200 bps from 22.9% to 3.5%; +100 bps from 10.9% to 1.8%), reflecting a materially downgraded/reduced economic exposure to rising rates. This is a genuine softening of the disclosed risk, not a wording change.

## ABSORBED

### 9. Model Simulation Methodology — EVE and NII Framework
`ITEM 7A` · conf 88%

**Year-1 Disclosure**
> A specific application of our simulation model involves measurement of the impact of changes in market interest rates on the economic value of equity EVE... Another application of the simulation model measures the impact of changes in market interest rates on NII assuming a static balance sheet.

**Year-2 Status**
> A specific application of our simulation model involves measurement of the impact of changes in market interest rates on the EVE... Another application of the simulation model measures the impact of changes in market interest rates on NII assuming a static balance sheet... Changes in market interest rates that affect NII are principally short-term interest rates and include the following benchmark indexes: (i) the National Prime Rate, (ii) SOFR rates, (iii) 1-month and 3-month LIBOR and (iv) the Federal Funds target rate.

**Analysis**
The same EVE/NII static-balance-sheet simulation methodology and the same underlying rate risk are fully retained, but the single Year-2021 unit was split across multiple Year-2022 disclosures (Candidates 1, 2, 3): the static balance sheet methodology, the benchmark index sensitivity (now adding SOFR), and the deposit beta assumptions. The economic risk is unchanged and arguably more granular, not softened or removed.

### 10. NII Simulation — Deposit Repricing, Rate Floors, and Decay Rate Assumptions
`ITEM 7A` · conf 88%

**Year-1 Disclosure**
> The 12-month NII simulations include repricing assumptions on our interest-bearing deposit products... The simulation model incorporates embedded rate floors on loans... we assume deposit balance decay rates based on a historical deposit study of our clients.

**Year-2 Status**
> The 12-month NII simulations include repricing assumptions on our interest-bearing deposit products of approximately 70 percent as of December 31, 2022... The simulation model incorporates embedded rate floors on loans, where present... we make certain deposit balance decay rate assumptions on demand deposits and interest-bearing deposits.

**Analysis**
The single Year-1 unit covering three risks (deposit repricing, rate floors, decay rate assumptions) was disaggregated and absorbed across three separate Year-2 disclosures (Candidates 1, 2, and 3); all three economic risks persist with comparable or expanded detail rather than being removed or softened.
